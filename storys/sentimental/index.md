# Sentimental by Xen

## Episode 1

The man at my door looked very sad. He was wearing an army outfit, like he too was a veteran.

'Sir, I've been sent here with bad news,' he said, 'your parents...'

I perked up

'They're dead.'

There was a long silence before he spoke;

'If you have anything to say... Or not...'

All I could do is hold back a smile.

'Well, feel free to...' he sighs, 'their funeral will be held this date and time, with further details enclosed' as he hands me a letter.

I take the letter with as straight of a face as I can manage and mumble something before heading back inside.

I look at the envelope for a second.

I place it neatly in a drawer of clothes I don't wear. I notice other things I was gifted. From friends I've since abandoned, my peers of which I haven't thought about in years...

And shut the drawer.

They mean nothing.

They're just items, they're not special.

Being a war veteran is hard... But being a sociopath is very much worse.

I hate it, but what more can I do?

## Episode 2

Breakfast is the same as usual. I cook 2 eggs, some bacon and seasoning. I put it between two slices of break to pretend I'm fancy. I'm not fancy, just making any attempt at emotion. They taste bland after eating the same thing so many times.

Someone calls my cell phone. I don't recognise the number. I let it go to voice mail as I finish eating and clean the dishes.

"Heyy! Just calling to confirm you're okay. Calling from a different number because my phone fell out of my pocket on a hike and I couldn't find it.'

I don't laugh.

'Remember to take your pills, and if you ever need something call this number instead of the old one. THank you!'

I turn on the television.

* * *

After what feels like an hour, I get up.

I turn the television off and head outside.

After checking the mail, I head to the grocery to buy whatever.

Nothing in the newspaper interests me. The food either looks too colourful or too bland.

I buy the same as I always do; more eggs, bacon and bread. Some easy dinners to cook and apples.

* * *

Once I get home, I haul the groceries onto the bench. I take a moment to think, before putting everything away.

It takes a while.

I don't know what to do with the rest of my day. I mostly just watch whatever's on the television.

The voicemail crosses over my mind once, before I forget about it again.

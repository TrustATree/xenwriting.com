# Onaduruun by Shadow

## Episode 1 — The Man at the Border

Earth — 2126

4:15 a.m.

Ferdinand opened his eyes.

The first thing he saw was concrete.

A cracked concrete ceiling stared back at him from above.

He remained still for several seconds, listening to the faint hum of the heating system.

It wasn't working particularly well.

Then again, almost nothing in Class 7 housing worked particularly well.

Ferdinand rolled out of bed and sat on the edge of the mattress.

His apartment was small.

One room.

One bed.

One table.

One barely functioning heater.

And rent that was becoming increasingly difficult to afford.

He had been told that he should have advanced to Class 6 housing by now.

Normally, his work credits would have been enough.

But the recent terrorist attacks had changed everything.

Credits were tighter.

Resources were being redirected.

People were being moved around.

And Ferdinand had been left behind.

He rubbed his eyes and looked at the clock.

4:15.

He had a shift at the border.

Of course he did.

Ferdinand stood, dressed for work, and left his apartment.

## The Border

By the time Ferdinand arrived, the immigration checkpoint was already busy.

People stretched into the distance.

Families.

Workers.

Travellers.

Refugees.

Entire groups waiting for their documents to be checked.

Ferdinand sat behind his desk.

The first person stepped forward.

"Identification."

The person handed him their documents.

Ferdinand checked them.

Approved.

Next.

Another set of documents.

Approved.

Next.

He continued.

One after another.

After years of working at the border, Ferdinand had become very good at processing people quickly.

He didn't ask unnecessary questions.

He didn't waste time.

He simply did his job.

The line kept moving.

Then someone stepped forward wearing clothes that immediately caught Ferdinand's attention.

The man's clothing looked completely out of place.

Expensive robes.

Unusual materials.

And hair so bright red that Ferdinand couldn't help staring.

His eyes were the same colour.

Ferdinand looked at him.

Then at the line behind him.

Then back at the man.

"Documents."

The man smiled.

"I don't have any."

Ferdinand stared.

"You don't have any?"

"No."

Ferdinand sighed.

"Then you can't enter."

The man didn't move.

Instead, he continued looking at Ferdinand.

"You have an interesting presence."

Ferdinand looked at the growing line behind him.

"Sir, please move aside."

"You've been through hell and back, haven't you?"

Ferdinand's expression hardened.

"Look, I don't know who you are, but you're holding up the line."

The stranger smiled.

"I can sense it."

"Sense what?"

"Potential."

Ferdinand almost laughed.

Potential.

That was a word he hadn't heard associated with himself in a very long time.

"Listen," Ferdinand said, "either get your papers sorted or get out of the line."

The stranger didn't seem offended.

If anything, he looked amused.

"You don't belong here."

Ferdinand frowned.

"What?"

"Not in this place."

The stranger reached into his robes.

Ferdinand immediately became more alert.

The man pulled out a strange piece of paper.

It didn't look like ordinary paper.

Symbols were drawn across its surface.

They seemed to shift whenever Ferdinand looked directly at them.

The stranger placed it on the desk.

"Come to my school."

Ferdinand stared at him.

"Your school?"

"I am the headmaster."

"Of what?"

"A magical institute."

Ferdinand almost laughed.

"Right."

The stranger smiled.

"You'll understand soon enough."

He turned and walked away.

Ferdinand looked down at the strange paper.

"Hey!"

The man didn't stop.

Ferdinand looked back at the line.

Hundreds of people were waiting.

He sighed.

"Whatever."

He put the paper aside.

He had no idea that touching it would change his life forever.

## Later That Day

Ferdinand returned home exhausted.

He threw his work bag onto the table.

The strange paper was still there.

He had brought it home.

He wasn't entirely sure why.

Something about it bothered him.

Ferdinand picked it up.

The symbols moved beneath his fingers.

He turned it over.

Nothing.

He turned it back.

Still nothing.

Then his fingers touched the centre.

The paper lit up.

Ferdinand jumped backwards.

"What the—"

The room disappeared.

For a moment, Ferdinand couldn't see anything.

He couldn't feel the floor.

He couldn't hear the heating system.

He couldn't hear the city outside.

Then—

WHOOSH.

His feet hit stone.

Ferdinand stumbled forward.

He caught himself against a desk.

He looked around.

Books.

Shelves.

Magical symbols.

Floating objects.

And standing across the room was the man from the border.

Ferdinand stared at him.

The man smiled.

"Welcome."

Ferdinand looked around.

"Where am I?"

"Onaduruun."

Ferdinand frowned.

"Where?"

"Onaduruun."

The man walked towards him.

"I am Sylvestor."

Ferdinand remembered the strange encounter at the border.

"The headmaster."

"Correct."

Ferdinand looked around again.

"So this is your school?"

"Yes."

"And you brought me here."

"Yes."

"Without asking."

Sylvestor smiled.

"You touched the paper."

Ferdinand stared at him.

"That is not the same thing."

Sylvestor laughed.

"You'll fit in perfectly."

Ferdinand wasn't convinced.

Sylvestor walked towards the window.

"Look."

Ferdinand reluctantly followed.

He looked outside.

His mouth fell open.

A city stretched beneath the window.

Massive buildings rose into the sky.

People walked through streets unlike anything Ferdinand had ever seen.

And magic was everywhere.

Ferdinand saw glowing lights floating above buildings.

He saw people carrying staffs.

He saw creatures he couldn't identify.

Then something enormous flew across the sky.

Ferdinand froze.

"Is that..."

Sylvestor nodded.

"A dragon."

Ferdinand stared.

He had no words.

Sylvestor smiled.

"Welcome to Onaduruun."

## The Scholarship

Sylvestor returned to his desk.

"I have been watching you."

Ferdinand frowned.

"That's slightly concerning."

"You possess extraordinary potential."

"That's what you said at the border."

"And I was correct."

Sylvestor placed several documents on the desk.

Ferdinand looked at them.

"What are these?"

"Your scholarship."

"My what?"

"A scholarship."

Ferdinand looked at Sylvestor.

"You want me to attend your school?"

"Yes."

"What school?"

Sylvestor smiled.

"The Magical Institute for Legends and Mythics."

Ferdinand stared at the documents.

"Legends and Mythics?"

"Only students with Legendary or Mythic affinity are normally accepted."

Ferdinand looked down at himself.

"My affinity isn't exactly impressive."

Sylvestor's expression changed.

"That's where you're wrong."

Ferdinand looked up.

Sylvestor studied him carefully.

"There is something inside you that I have never sensed before."

"What?"

"I don't know."

That answer caught Ferdinand off guard.

"You don't know?"

Sylvestor shook his head.

"I have lived a very long time. I have studied magic for longer than most people can imagine."

He paused.

"But your magic..."

Sylvestor looked at Ferdinand's silver-grey hair and eyes.

"...is different."

Ferdinand remained silent.

"Your appearance suggests Common affinity," Sylvestor continued.

"Common?"

"That is what most people would assume."

"And what do you think?"

Sylvestor smiled.

"I think they are wrong."

Ferdinand looked at the scholarship.

He didn't understand why this stranger believed in him.

He didn't understand why he had been brought here.

He didn't even understand what Onaduruun was.

But for the first time in years, someone was offering him an opportunity.

Not another shift.

Not another government assignment.

Not another bill.

An opportunity.

Ferdinand picked up the scholarship.

"So what happens if I say yes?"

Sylvestor smiled.

"Then your life changes."

Ferdinand looked at the strange world outside.

He thought about his tiny apartment.

The broken heater.

The endless border queues.

The life he had been trapped inside.

Then he looked at the scholarship again.

"Fine."

Sylvestor raised an eyebrow.

"Fine?"

"I'll go."

Sylvestor smiled.

"Excellent."

Ferdinand stood.

"One question."

"Yes?"

"Can I go back to Earth?"

Sylvestor's smile faded slightly.

"Eventually."

Ferdinand sighed.

"There it is."

"What?"

"The catch."

Sylvestor laughed.

"You'll understand soon."

Ferdinand looked out the window again.

He didn't know it yet, but he had just taken the first step into a world of magic, guilds, kingdoms and powers far beyond anything he had ever imagined.

And somewhere inside him, something ancient had begun to awaken.

Something even Sylvestor couldn't identify.

Something that would eventually change Onaduruun forever.
